County Permit Pipeline

A nightly ingest of building-permit data across eleven Sunbelt counties, normalized into a single schema and pushed into the acquisitions team’s dashboard the next morning at 6am.

§ 01The problem

Acquisitions teams in build-for-rent run on permit data. A new single-family subdivision permit in a target market is the cleanest leading signal you get — six to eighteen months before the homes are competing with us. The catch is that every county publishes the data differently, on a different cadence, in a different format. Half of them think a weekly CSV emailed to a list is acceptable distribution. (It is not.)

Before this project, our team checked three to four county sites manually per week. The signal was real; the work was tedious; the coverage was uneven.

§ 02The shape of the thing

One Python service, scheduled hourly on Railway, with eleven county-specific adapters that all return rows shaped like a common PermitRecord. Records land in Supabase Postgres with PostGIS, get geocoded against parcel boundaries we already had, and get tagged against our acquisition buy-box. Apps Script fans out a 6am digest into the team’s shared Google Sheet so nothing changes about how they actually work.

§ 03Three decisions that mattered

  1. One adapter per county, not a clever framework. I tried to factor out a base class twice. Both times a county changed its export format inside a month and I had to undo it. The boring solution — eleven 80-line files that share nothing but a return type — has paid for itself every quarter.
  2. Geocode against our parcel layer, not Google. The records arrive with addresses, but addresses lie. Joining to parcels we already trust means a downstream user can click a row and immediately see the polygon. This single change eliminated the “is this the same lot?” question that used to start every acquisitions meeting.
  3. Stay inside the spreadsheet. The team lives in Google Sheets. A dashboard nobody opens is worse than a stale tab they already have open. The digest is just a sheet, written to by Apps Script, with hyperlinks back into the dashboard for the cases where someone wants more.

§ 04What it has done

Eighteen months in, the pipeline has surfaced four sites we bought, two we passed on early enough to save the underwriting effort, and exactly one weekend where the Maricopa feed went down and I got to learn how the team feels when it isn’t there. That third number is the one that told me to write the alerting.

§ 06What I’d do differently

I would start with the parcel join, not bolt it on in v 1.4. I would write the alerting on day one, even if there is nothing to alert on yet. And I would resist the very strong urge to build a proper web UI for a tool whose users are perfectly happy in a Google Sheet.
